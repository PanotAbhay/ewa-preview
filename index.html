Who the groups are for

EwA started as a group for adults who have experienced, or are experiencing, mental health difficulties. People come to express what they feel, to ease isolation and to meet others who understand.

The groups also welcome people with experiences such as:

  • ADHD, dyslexia and other kinds of neurodivergence
  • surviving domestic abuse
  • post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and other trauma

What happens at a group

  1. Arrive and settle in. There is food and something to drink.
  2. Make art your way. Painting, drawing and other materials. All abilities are welcome.
  3. Meet people. Members support each other. You can talk, or just make.

Small steps can lead to bigger steps

Phillip used these words in 2013 to describe the group. EwA is now shaping them into an approach with two stages. You move at your own pace, and you choose how far to go.

  1. Stage one Recovery

    Make art in a relaxed group. Express feelings that are hard to put into words. Build confidence and friendships, with people who understand.

  2. Stage two Reimmersion

    When you are ready, use your creativity to reconnect with the wider world. Show and sell your work, take on creative projects, and explore flexible work such as freelancing or a small creative business.

The two stages are a proposed approach. Confirm the names, what support each stage includes, and which parts run today.

A group that began on the Isle of Dogs

Phillip Singh started the group in 2013, with funding from NHS East London. He wanted a place where people could express themselves through art, as he does. Since then, members have helped to paint a mural with the residents of a women's hostel, and shown their work at exhibitions and events.

Need help now?

EwA is not a crisis service. If you or someone else is in danger, call 999. For urgent mental health help in England, call NHS 111 and choose option 2. You can talk to Samaritans free at any time on 116 123.
