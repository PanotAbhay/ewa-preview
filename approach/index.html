Small steps can lead to bigger steps

EwA uses art to support wellbeing. First, a safe place to make art and recover. Then, when you are ready, a way to use your creativity in the wider world.

  1. Stage one Recovery

    The weekly group is the start. You make art in a relaxed place, with people who understand. Art gives you a way to express feelings that are hard to say in words.

    • make art at your own pace, with no pressure to finish
    • meet people and feel less alone
    • build your confidence and self-esteem
    • visit galleries and see your work shown

  2. Stage two Reimmersion

    Some members want more. Reimmersion means using your creativity to reconnect with work and the wider world, in a way that suits your life.

    • show and sell your artwork
    • take part in commissions, such as murals
    • learn about freelancing and flexible work
    • explore starting a small creative business

    The support for stage two is not yet designed. Confirm which activities run today and which are plans.

What this approach means for you

  • You choose. The stages are not a course. You do not have to move to stage two, and you can come back to stage one at any time.
  • There is no timetable. People come and go as they recover. Nobody is pushed.
  • Members lead. The group is run by its members, and decisions are made together.

What EwA does not do

  • EwA does not give therapy, diagnosis or medical treatment.
  • EwA is not a crisis service. See the numbers at the bottom of each page.
  • EwA cannot promise work, income or a business result.

Where the idea comes from

When the group started in 2013, Phillip Singh said that “small steps can lead to bigger steps”. He also said that “people will come and go as they recover and resume their previous lives”.

The two stages are a way to put those words into practice. Art helps people recover. Art can also be a skill, a source of pride and, for some people, a way back into work.

Need help now?

EwA is not a crisis service. If you or someone else is in danger, call 999. For urgent mental health help in England, call NHS 111 and choose option 2. You can talk to Samaritans free at any time on 116 123.
